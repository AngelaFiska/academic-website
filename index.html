Angela Faragasso, PhD

Assistant Professor of Computer Science
Temple University, Japan Campus

What I love about robotics is the chance to build something that can help people. I’ve worked on navigation for mine rescue robots, built a probe to measure the stiffness of soft tissue, and studied how robots can help with nuclear cleanup in Japan.

At Temple, I teach software design and computer systems. I especially enjoy the moment a student can explain an idea they found difficult just a little while ago.

Background

I studied robotics at Sapienza University of Rome and did my PhD at King’s College London. In Japan, I held a JSPS fellowship at the University of Tokyo and later worked as a lead research engineer at FingerVision.

Real-Time Vision-Based Stiffness Mapping

During my PhD, I built a handheld probe that used a camera to track how its tips moved against a soft surface. Taking readings at several points made it possible to map differences in stiffness.

Play with a sample

Can you find the stiff spot? Tap the gel in a few places. Each tap presses the probe 2 mm into the sample. A firmer spot takes more force. You can also drag down to press deeper.

About this model

Each position acts like a spring. Local stiffness is the force divided by indentation depth. Taps and the press button use a depth of 2 mm. The dots record positions you have sampled. This is an illustration inspired by my work on vision-based stiffness mapping.

Teaching

My goal is to make difficult concepts easy to understand. One of my favourite moments is hearing a student explain something in their own words after it has finally clicked.

CIS 2107

Computer Systems and Low-Level Programming

We follow a C program down to the machine level. Students read assembly, trace pointers and work out what happens in memory when a program runs.

CIS 3296

Software Design

Students build a piece of software together over the semester. At each sprint, they demonstrate what works and talk through a design decision with the class.
